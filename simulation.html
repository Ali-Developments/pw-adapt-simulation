PW-ADAPT
Graduation project proposal · produced-water treatment

A treatment route chosen for each water, not one train for every water

PW-ADAPT reads sensors on produced water, predicts scaling, toxicity and corrosion risk, picks the cheapest treatment route that meets the end-use limits, explains the choice, and drives a bench rig only after an engineer approves.

The problem

  • Produced water changes with the well and over time: oil, solids, salt and scaling ions all move.
  • A fixed treatment train is sized for the worst case, so easy water pays for treatment it does not need.
  • Automation in the field mostly tunes single units by hand or with unit-level control.

What PW-ADAPT adds

  • Route-level choice across several modules, not tuning of one unit.
  • Explainability: every risk score and route comes with its drivers (SHAP).
  • OPEX and end-use matching: cheapest route that meets reinjection, cooling or irrigation limits.
  • Human in the loop with hardware interlocks that no software fault can override.

What it does not claim

  • It is a proof of concept for decision logic, about 3,200 to 6,500 times smaller than a real plant.
  • Raw brine above about 70 g/L cannot be desalinated by the rig; RO runs on diluted or surrogate water only.
  • The oil sensor is a proxy, calibrated against lab oil-and-grease.
  • Module efficiencies in the simulator are placeholders until bench data replaces them.

Objectives and deliverables

  • O1. Classify water state and predict scaling, toxicity and corrosion risk (macro F1 ≥ 0.85 in cross-validation, ≥ 0.75 on real held-out samples).
  • O2. Model removal efficiency and cost per module from measured inlet and outlet samples (error ≤ 10 percentage points).
  • O3. Recommend the cheapest route that satisfies every end-use limit, with OPEX range and SHAP explanation.
  • O4. Prove the loop on a bench rig: sense, decide, approve, treat, verify, re-plan, with zero unsafe actuations.
  • O5. Show at least 20% lower OPEX than a fixed full train where a shorter route is enough, and 30% lower than modelled disposal at the median.
  • O6. Report every KPI honestly in a paper, including those not met.

Rig, sensors and budget

A small rig that is honest about what cheap sensors can do

Parts are chosen for brine and oil, with a hardware safety layer that works even if the firmware or Wi-Fi fails.

Process

  • 10–20 L raw and treated tanks in a containment tray
  • Air-diffuser flotation chamber (PP or HDPE)
  • Gravity media column: sand, anthracite or carbon, with backflush port
  • Optional household mini-RO, diluted or surrogate water only
  • 6 normally-closed solenoid valves: inlet and bypass per module

Sensing and control

  • K = 10 conductivity probe with isolated EZO circuit (inlet)
  • pH, turbidity, temperature, flow and pressure at inlet and outlet
  • Optical oil proxy, calibrated against lab oil-and-grease
  • ESP32 with ADS1115 converters and relay boards
  • Energy meter at the 12 V supply

Hardware safety layer

  • Emergency stop in series with actuator power
  • Float switches in the relay control circuit
  • Mechanical relief valve below the weakest component
  • Normally-closed valves and relays default off on power loss
  • Limits fixed in firmware, never settable over MQTT

Sensor simulator

Shows why raw readings cannot drive decisions: noise, drift and fouling are added to a true value, then the firmware's median-of-5 filter and quality flags are applied.

Calibration against lab values (12 samples)

Target for the oil proxy: R² ≥ 0.80 against lab oil-and-grease. Noise and fouling lower it; recalibrating each run day is part of the protocol.

Bill of materials (EGP, Egyptian market)

Prices are the plan's listed or estimated values, not quotes. About 10% exchange-rate movement is possible, and shipping and VAT are excluded.

Gaps found in review, fixed in this design

  • Relay channels: 10–13 loads need two 8-channel boards or one 16-channel board.
  • Analog inputs: 9 signals need three ADS1115 boards.
  • Tank size: a 600 s step at 2.1 L/min moves about 21 L, so use 25 L tanks or recirculate.
  • Outlet conductivity and oil sensing are drawn, so they are bought, or borrowed from the faculty lab.
  • Valves at oil-exposed or low-pressure points are direct-acting with FKM or PTFE seals.
  • Probes that share a liquid are isolated or placed in separate cells.

Approval path

1 · Recommend

AI proposes a route. No actuator moves.

2 · Approve

Engineer clicks Approve. Backend signs a token with an expiry.

3 · Check

ESP32 verifies signature, expiry and an increasing command number. Forged, expired and replayed commands are rejected.

4 · Run

Valves and pumps move; heartbeat every 2 s, safe state after 6 s of silence.

The cabinet is breadboard-level and is not safety-certified. Real produced water can carry H2S, hydrocarbons, heavy metals and NORM, so it runs only with written permission and a disposal route.

Main risks and Plan B

  • Real water not allowed: lab jar tests plus surrogate water spiked to measured values.
  • Lab delay or few samples: start on PHREEQC and literature; create range by dilution and label it.
  • Oil sensor drifts: spare, turbidity proxy, recalibrate each run.
  • Seals or tubing attacked: FKM or PTFE, PP, PVDF or 316 stainless; flush after each run.
  • RO unusable: drop it or treat it as simulated.

Minimum viable version

  • XGBoost risk model with SHAP
  • Two modules: flotation and media filter
  • Enumeration route choice, Streamlit dashboard with approval
  • ESP32 driving two valves and one pump
  • Three repeated scenarios, one with real water and lab verification

What we ask for

  • Core rig budget: 30,278–46,657 EGP with contingency (optional items up to 18,300 EGP more)
  • Lab quotes per sample for ICP, oil and grease, alkalinity and anions (not in budget)
  • Real produced-water samples and written permission to run them
  • Loan of a calibrated high-range conductivity meter if the faculty lab has one

Route optimizer (Python)


from itertools import product
from pydantic import BaseModel

class Quality(BaseModel):
    oil_mg_l: float; turbidity_ntu: float
    tds_mg_l: float; ph: float

def recommend(feed: Quality, end_use: str, models, limits):
    best = None
    for mask in product([0, 1], repeat=3):          # flotation, filter, RO
        q, cost, ok = feed, 0.0, True
        for use, mod in zip(mask, models):
            if not use:
                continue
            if not mod.feasible(q):                   # e.g. RO feed TDS
                ok = False; break
            q = mod.predict(q)                        # GPR mean
            cost += mod.cost_usd_m3
        ok = ok and all(getattr(q, k) <= v * 0.95
                        for k, v in limits[end_use].items())
        if ok and (best is None or cost < best.cost):
            best = Route(mask, q, cost)
    return best    # None means: no route on this rig works

    
Edge safety loop (ESP32, C++)


void loop() {
  if (digitalRead(PIN_ESTOP_SENSE) == LOW) enterSafe("ESTOP");
  if (millis() - lastHeartbeat > 6000)    enterSafe("HEARTBEAT");
  if (readPressureKpa() > P_LIMIT_KPA)    enterSafe("OVERPRESSURE");
  if (stepRunning && millis() - stepStart > stepMaxMs)
                                           enterSafe("STEP_TIMEOUT");
  if (criticalSensorInvalid()) {
    if (holdSince == 0) holdSince = millis();
    if (millis() - holdSince > 10000)      enterSafe("SENSOR");
  } else holdSince = 0;
  esp_task_wdt_reset();                    // hardware watchdog
}

void onCommand(Cmd c) {                    // from MQTT
  if (!verifySig(c.token) || c.expires < now() ||
      c.id <= lastCmdId || state != IDLE) { ack(c, "rejected"); return; }
  lastCmdId = c.id; startRoute(c); ack(c, "accepted");
}
// Limits (P_LIMIT_KPA, stepMaxMs) live in firmware; MQTT cannot change them.
